# Salita Quest v4.2 — Design research notes

The Journey Edition applies gamification as a support for learning rather than an end in itself.

## Design principles used

1. **Autonomy, competence, and relatedness.** A 2025 Scientific Reports study on online language learning found social reinforcement could support motivation through the self-determination dimensions of autonomy, competence, and relatedness. Salita Quest therefore emphasizes visible competence, learner-selected review, and supportive feedback rather than punishment.
2. **Small daily goals.** Daily quests are deliberately short and varied: finish a session, achieve several correct retrievals, and strengthen learned material. This avoids making raw XP accumulation the primary task.
3. **Habit without excessive punishment.** The streak remains visible, while weekly momentum also rewards returning learners. This follows evidence and product experimentation suggesting that demanding streak thresholds can undermine adherence.
4. **Retrieval and feedback remain primary.** Game elements wrap around the existing spaced-retrieval loop; they do not replace it. Corrective feedback and review scheduling continue to determine mastery progression.
5. **Celebration is proportional.** Small answers receive subtle feedback; larger animations are reserved for meaningful milestones such as a strong session or completing all daily quests.
6. **Choice and accessibility.** Learners can disable motion, celebration sounds, or use dark mode. Topic review and dictionary practice remain self-directed.

## Sources consulted

- Zhang Q, Shakibaei G. Using social reinforcement in online Language learning to foster motivation through self-determination theory. Scientific Reports. 2025;15:34944. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-18953-4
- Duolingo. Improving the streak. https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak/
- Duolingo. Putting in work: The habit of language learning. https://blog.duolingo.com/putting-in-work-the-habit-of-language-learning/

The interface is an original Salita Quest design. It borrows general mobile-learning conventions such as a visual journey, bite-sized goals, immediate feedback, and progress indicators rather than copying a specific commercial interface.
