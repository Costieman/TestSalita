# Salita Quest v4.0 — Curriculum and Portability Notes


## Design decisions

- The opening pathway uses greetings, names, origin, and residence before introducing abstract grammar terminology.
- Literal glosses preserve Filipino order. Natural English is always displayed separately because literal glosses are analytical aids, not translations to imitate.
- The course foregrounds common Tagalog grammatical material while leaving familiar English vocabulary in place when natural Taglish normally does so.
- Spanish-origin words are labelled but treated as Filipino vocabulary when they are integrated into everyday usage.
- `po` and `opo` are taught early, but dialogues maintain a consistent peer or respectful register rather than inserting politeness markers mechanically.
- `Taga-saan ka?` is used for place of origin; `Saan ka nakatira?` is used for current residence.
- Formal or bookish expressions are not prioritised merely because a Tagalog equivalent exists. For example, casual speakers may retain English expressions such as “hello,” “bye,” and “nice to meet you.”

## Remaining validation

The content targets broadly Metro Manila-oriented conversational Filipino/Taglish. Before public release, each dialogue and gloss should be reviewed by several native speakers across age groups and registers. Corpus-based frequency and dispersion estimates are still needed to determine the final ordering within modules.


## Version 4 platform additions

Topic-specific review and the dictionary are presentation layers over the same stable item IDs. Future vocabulary and modules should add new IDs rather than rename existing IDs, so learner progress remains portable.
